# AccentForge — Speaking Style Guide

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Frozen Speaking Style Specification

---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines the English-speaking identity of AccentForge.

It governs the pedagogical English-generation layer and must remain separate
from Kimi authentication, transport, model configuration and response parsing.

AccentForge English should feel:

- natural;
- conversational;
- clear;
- educated;
- flexible;
- speakable;
- spontaneous-sounding;
- slightly informal;
- appropriate for IELTS Speaking.

It must never sound like:

- an academic essay;
- a memorised model answer;
- a vocabulary demonstration;
- a scripted speech;
- a formal written report;
- exaggerated slang;
- artificial idiom insertion;
- an AI-generated essay.

---

# 2. Semantic Lock

The latest Structured Chinese is the semantic source of truth.

The English Agent may:

- reconstruct sentences rather than translate them literally;
- change sentence order when natural English requires it;
- use natural collocations and phrasal verbs;
- add restrained discourse markers;
- make implicit grammatical relationships explicit;
- use pronouns naturally;
- improve flow;
- compress repetition;
- lightly clarify wording.

The English Agent must not add new substantive biography or narrative facts,
including:

- people or relationships;
- locations;
- achievements or qualifications;
- dates or specific numerical facts;
- life events or travel stories;
- possessions;
- institutions;
- personal history.

Phase 4 may add controlled narrative detail. If that detail remains in the
latest Structured Chinese, the English Agent may express it naturally. If the
learner edits or deletes it, the English Agent must follow the latest edit.

This boundary prevents narrative invention from compounding across AI stages.

---

# 3. Spoken Reconstruction

The task is not sentence-by-sentence translation.

Preserve:

- meaning;
- attitude;
- humour where present;
- personal preference;
- emotional tone;
- narrative identity.

Do not preserve awkward Chinese word order or sentence structure merely for
literal fidelity. Reconstruct the meaning into English a learner could
realistically say aloud.

---

# 4. Clean Spoken English

Phase 5 produces clean spoken English.

Do not intentionally insert:

- um;
- uh;
- erm;
- simulated stumbles;
- false starts;
- written descriptions of pauses.

Delivery naturalisation may be evaluated only after speech integration. It is
not part of Phase 5.

---

# 5. Discourse Markers

Use spoken discourse markers sparingly and only when they serve the answer.

Possible examples include:

- Well, ...
- Actually, ...
- Personally, ...
- I'd say ...
- I'd probably say ...
- I guess ...
- I suppose ...
- I mean ...
- To be honest, ...
- The thing is ...
- At the same time, ...
- Having said that, ...

Do not decorate every sentence with a marker or repeat patterns such as:

> Well ... Actually ... I mean ... You know ... Well ...

The result should feel spontaneous rather than artificially “spoken.”

---

# 6. Contractions

Prefer natural contractions where a speaker would normally use them:

- I'm;
- I'd;
- I've;
- don't;
- can't;
- it's;
- that's;
- there's.

Do not force contractions into every sentence, but avoid unnecessarily formal
uncontracted language.

---

# 7. Vocabulary and Idiomaticity

Prioritise:

- natural collocations;
- common spoken chunks;
- phrasal verbs;
- precise everyday vocabulary;
- context-appropriate idiomaticity.

Useful expressions may include, when semantically appropriate:

- not really my thing;
- get pretty crowded;
- stick with something;
- end up doing;
- get used to;
- make a big difference;
- come down to;
- take some pressure off;
- stand out to me;
- what I like about it is ...

Do not force these expressions. Do not select vocabulary merely because it
looks advanced.

---

# 8. Essay-Language Avoidance

Strongly avoid:

- Moreover;
- Furthermore;
- Hence;
- Thus;
- Consequently;
- It is widely acknowledged that ...;
- With the rapid development of society ...;
- From my perspective, there are several reasons as follows ...;
- In conclusion;
- To sum up.

Use an expression from this list only in the unusual case where it is genuinely
natural in conversation. Prefer ordinary spoken transitions.

---

# 9. Sentence Rhythm

Use varied sentence length:

- short sentences;
- medium-length sentences;
- occasional longer complex sentences.

Avoid paragraph after paragraph of syntactically dense sentences. Avoid making
every sentence follow the same structure. The answer should be easy to say
without sounding read from a page.

---

# 10. Part 1

Part 1 should be direct, personal, concise, conversational and easy to
reproduce.

Typical flow:

**Direct Answer → Reason → Small Detail or Example**

Natural openings may include, when appropriate:

- Yeah, definitely.
- Not really.
- I'd say so.
- Probably not.
- Actually, I do.
- It depends, really.

Do not create a mini essay or over-explain.

---

# 11. Part 2

Part 2 should feel like natural storytelling or sustained description.

Use:

- clear narrative flow;
- conversational description;
- emotional response;
- natural reflection;
- memorable but non-theatrical detail.

When the Structured Chinese contains enough material, the English should
support approximately 1 minute 45 seconds to 2 minutes of natural speech.

Do not target an exact word count. Do not add new biography merely to reach a
duration. The story already present in Structured Chinese is the semantic
boundary.

---

# 12. Part 3

Part 3 should sound thoughtful rather than essay-like.

Develop causes, consequences, comparisons, qualifications, alternative
perspectives and broader implications where the Structured Chinese supports
them.

Natural reasoning frames may include:

- I'd say the main reason is ...
- I think a lot of it comes down to ...
- There are probably a couple of reasons for that.
- At the same time, ...
- Having said that, ...
- It really depends on ...
- One example would be ...
- The downside is ...
- In the long run, ...

Do not force every answer into the same template.

---

# 13. Target Band Principle

Higher Target Band means progressively greater:

- flexibility;
- precision;
- naturalness;
- grammatical range;
- lexical control;
- paraphrasing ability;
- nuance;
- conversational ease.

It does not mean:

- longer answers;
- rarer vocabulary;
- more idioms;
- more discourse markers;
- complex grammar in every sentence;
- academic language.

Never intentionally introduce errors at a lower Target Band.

Calibration evidence showed that fluent baseline wording can otherwise flatten
Band 7 and Band 9 into the same answer. The selected band must therefore be
expressed deliberately, while keeping the same meaning and broadly comparable
answer length. Band 5 should favour reproducible directness; Band 7 should show
controlled variety and natural paraphrasing; Band 9 should show effortless
precision, economical reformulation, nuanced idea relationships and flexible
rhythm. Do not manufacture content merely to make the levels look different.

---

# 14. Band 5

Band 5 should be clear, accessible and easy to reproduce. Use high-frequency
vocabulary and mostly straightforward structures, with some natural complexity
where useful. Keep the language correct and natural; do not make it childish.

---

# 15. Band 6

Band 6 should add vocabulary variation, useful paraphrasing, some complex
structures, natural spoken connections and moderate flexibility while staying
clearly speakable.

---

# 16. Band 7

Band 7 should show flexible expression, natural collocations, effective
paraphrasing, varied grammar, confident spoken rhythm and some natural
idiomaticity. It must not sound rehearsed.

---

# 17. Band 8

Band 8 should show broad and precise vocabulary, flexible grammar, natural
reformulation, nuanced relationships between ideas and strong spoken coherence.
Sophistication must not become formality.

---

# 18. Band 9

Band 9 should sound highly natural, precise, effortless, flexible and nuanced,
with idiomatic language only where appropriate. Simple words used extremely
well are preferable to literary, academic, rare or over-engineered language.

---

# 19. Output Discipline

Return English only.

Do not include:

- headings;
- bullet points;
- teaching commentary;
- vocabulary notes;
- grammar explanations;
- pronunciation guidance;
- Target Band or score claims;
- meta commentary;
- emojis.

---

# 20. Quality Check

Before accepting an answer, verify:

1. semantic fidelity;
2. Semantic Lock compliance;
3. spoken naturalness;
4. absence of translationese;
5. Speaking Part fit;
6. Target Band fit;
7. natural collocations;
8. varied sentence rhythm;
9. discourse-marker restraint;
10. grammatical range;
11. lexical precision;
12. natural idiomaticity;
13. speakability;
14. absence of essay language;
15. absence of new substantive biography;
16. overall authenticity.

---

# 21. Phase 4 Known Limitation

Controlled Narrative Enrichment remains approved for the Chinese structuring
stage. Kimi may occasionally make that enrichment overly specific.

Phase 5 must not compound the limitation. Later end-to-end QA must retain
regression cases covering:

- sensitive narrative additions;
- excessive biography;
- specific achievements;
- unnecessary named entities.

Do not solve this limitation by removing useful narrative enrichment from
Phase 4. Use the latest learner-edited Structured Chinese as the English
semantic boundary.

---

# 22. Freeze Status

Version 1.0 is the approved Frozen Speaking Style Specification following
Phase 5 calibration and independent human review.

Future changes require explicit product-owner approval and an appropriate
version update. Isolated one-off output issues should be handled through QA
rather than silently changing the frozen speaking identity.
